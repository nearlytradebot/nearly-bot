# Wallets and security

## Bot wallets (Telegram and X)

Bot wallets are **custodial**: Nearly Bot holds their keys so it can launch, trade and bridge when you ask.

- Each wallet is a NEAR account with its own ed25519 key, generated by the bot. New wallets are implicit
  accounts (a 64-character address) that activate with their first deposit.
- Keys are **encrypted at rest** (AES-GCM) with a key that only the Nearly Bot service holds.
- Keys are **never shown in chat** and never sent to the website.
- **Telegram**: several wallets per user; everything uses the active one. `/import` adds a wallet you already
  own; the bot deletes your message with the key right away and checks it's a full-access key on chain.
- **X**: exactly one wallet per X account.
- **Custom names** (`alice.nearlytradebot.near`): the new account gets the same key as your wallet, your NEAR
  moves over, and tokens left at the old address can be moved with one tap.

Keep only what you're actively using in a bot wallet. Withdraw the rest to a wallet you control.

## Website wallets

On the website you connect **your own** NEAR wallet (Meteor, HOT, MyNearWallet, HERE, Intear, Sender, OKX,
Nightly, NEAR Mobile, Ledger, MetaMask, Rabby or WalletConnect). Nearly Bot never sees its keys: every swap,
bridge and name payment is approved in your wallet.

- Telegram wallets aren't linked to the website.
- **Connect X** shows your X bot wallet's address and balance after you sign in with X. It's view only: the
  website can't move funds from it.

## What Nearly Bot can't do

- Change a token after launch: supply, pool and tax are fixed by the factory.
- Pull liquidity: the pool belongs to the nearly.trade factory.
- Touch tokens or NEAR in a wallet you connect on the website without your approval.
